The leading space in `scanf(" %c", &ch)` skips all whitespace before reading one character, which behaves like C++'s `cin >> ch`; reading stops at `#`. Only the first n letters are counted.

`ranked` is a copy of the counts sorted in decreasing order with `qsort` and the `descending` comparator. The global `take(a, b)` scans from A for the first unused worker whose count is a or b, which selects the smallest letter currently allowed.

Each left tier picks one letter and stores the count needed on the right in `right_need[k]`; for odd N the middle takes the smallest count; the right half is filled into `order[n-1-k]` from the innermost tier outward. `count` and `used` are reset before every case so nothing leaks from the previous one. Output uses `%c` or `%d` followed by a space or newline, so there is no trailing space.
