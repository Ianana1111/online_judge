C has no built-in queue, so the program uses an array of length 2n with two indices, `head` and `tail`: `head` points at the top of the deck and `tail` is where the next card moved to the bottom will go. Each round removes two cards from the front and adds one at the back, so `tail` advances at most n − 1 times and 2n slots always suffice without wrap-around.

It prints `Discarded cards:` first, then uses `" %d"` for the first card and `", %d"` afterwards, chosen by the `first` flag. Discarding is `deck[head++]`, and moving a card to the bottom is the single statement `deck[tail++] = deck[head++]`.

The loop stops when `tail - head < 2`, and `deck[head]` is the last card. The array is freed after each case so memory does not accumulate across many inputs.
