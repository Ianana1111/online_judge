# Follow the carry from one column to the next

## Problem and constraints

Add two nonnegative integers, each with fewer than ten digits, and count the columns that generate a carry. Read pairs until `0 0`; one zero paired with a nonzero number is an ordinary case. The required output distinguishes zero, one, and multiple carry operations.

## Building the approach

Imagine adding the numbers on paper. The units column is independent of all columns to its left, but the tens column depends on whether the units column produced a carry. This tells us both the processing order and the only state we need to remember.

Extract the current digits with remainder modulo ten. Their sum must include the incoming carry. If that total is at least ten, this column produces a new carry: increment the answer and carry one into the next column. Otherwise the outgoing carry becomes zero. Divide both numbers by ten to move left.

Continue while either number still has digits. A shorter number simply contributes zero. Do not count the final leading carry again: the operation that created it was already counted in the previous column.

## Walkthrough

For `999 + 1`, the units total is 10, the tens total is 9 + 0 + 1 = 10, and the hundreds total is also 10. Three different columns produce carries, so the answer is `3 carry operations.` For `123 + 594`, only the tens column produces a carry, giving `1 carry operation.`

## Why it works

Before each iteration, `carry` equals the carry generated by the immediately lower column. Adding it to the two current digits therefore reproduces ordinary addition exactly. The sum is at most 19, so the outgoing carry is always zero or one. Counting every column whose total reaches ten counts every carry operation once.

## Complexity

O(D) time for D digits in the longer number and O(1) extra space.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the incoming carry when examining a column.
- Stopping when either number runs out of digits.
- Treating any pair containing zero as the sentinel.
- Adding an extra operation for the final leading digit.
- Printing the plural form for exactly one operation.
